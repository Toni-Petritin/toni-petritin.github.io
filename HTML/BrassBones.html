Prelude

Screaming. Endless screaming.

Is that me screaming? Why would i be screaming? Am i in pain? Argh, my brain is so foggy! Why? Why? WHY?

I force my brain to function. I open my eye—only one. Shouldn't i have more eyes? Should i? I have difficulty parsing what i am seeing around me. A room... I am on a wall? How can i be lying on a wall?

An involuntarily dry cough stops my screaming, and i start to breathe. The tethers keeping me attached to the wall let go, and i fall limply to the steel floor. Why did it make such a loud sound? I move my right arm weakly. Should i have more limbs than this? My brain hurts and there is something wrong with the arm i am moving... Something wrong with everything in me!

I try to push myself up, but i am instantly unbalanced and fall on my side. Lying there, i recognize a dead body sitting against a wall just few meters away from me. I decide to push myself to it, lacking any other focus point.

My movements feel like i am encased in water. Something heavy drags behind me, and i can't seem to balance myself properly while pushing myself onward with my right arm. Why right arm and not just arm, if i only have one?

Something slick drips onto the floor from my face as i move. Should i be alarmed by the liquid? I feel like i should, but i can not think why. Is it bad that it is dripping out of me?

I reach the corpse and slump against it in exhaustion—getting to it felt like it took an eternity. I concentrate on the icy cold steel floor against my naked skin to keep myself conscious... Or maybe i should just let myself sleep...

The body moves. It rasps and labors to draw a breath under my head. I am alarmed by the sudden movement of the dead thing, but i can hardly force my arm to respond. I manage to turn my head toward the sounds it is making. My vision is foggy. Should i understand the sounds the corpse is making? There seems to be a pattern, but i can't keep hold of it—the sounds are too quiet and come to an end too fast.

The corpse is pushing something towards my face. I have a face? I react instinctively and take hold of the item before it reaches me. The hands of the dead thing fall to the ground, and it lets out a breath. I get the impression it is trying to tell me something, but is too weak.

I notice the item the corpse was holding is still in my hand. It seems to be partly lodged in me. Was i too slow? Did it kill me already? My thoughts are a confusing jumbled mess, but i notice that the item seems to easily slide deeper into my face. It fits perfectly below my right eye. I get a sudden revelation! I have a right eye as well? Maybe this thing is part of me? Maybe it is something that i am missing?

I start pushing the metal slab into my head. When i meet resistance, i need to rest my arm a little before pushing it the final few millimeters. There is a loud click as the slab locks in place. It is followed by a quiet, fast-paced clicking somewhere near the slab and an almost imperceptive high-pitched metallic whine deeper inside my head.

A word appears in the vision of my right eye—the one that i hadn't even realized existed until a few moments ago. It reads simply:

Initializing...

The word is instantly replaced by a set of new ones.

Allotting vitae to cortex functions...

Below these, new ones start appearing in a rapid stream, too fast for me to make sense of.


Chapter 1

The Assembly

As suddenly as i woke up tied to the wall a few minutes ago, i suddenly become I. The fog around my mind disappears and I become aware of... more, much more. And I also feel a lot more—all of it bad. I have a skull-splitting headache, my throat hurts like I have coughed up needles, my muscles ache of misuse and I generally just feel really sickly.

The massive sensory assault and the continuous information from the stream of words in my vision threaten to overwhelm me. I focus desperately on the first thing in front of me in an effort to push aside my newly acquired sense of the torturous reality.

The man whose lap i had laid my head on and who I am now staring at is not dead—not yet at least. My earlier mind had recognized the amount of blood on his body and concluded in its hazy state the man must have already died. And something changing from dead to living just because it moved had apparently been too big of a leap to it... to me.

The man has a weak but reassuring smile on his face. It takes me a few moments to recall how to translate his expression more keenly; he seems... proud... of me? Confused, I go through the events of the last few minutes, or rather the events of my entire memory. Had I done something to be proud of without knowing it?

I realize suddenly that the man had said, or whispered, something while I had been immersed in thought. I try to speak, ask him to repeat himself, but the dryness in my throat throws me into a painful coughing fit. When my lungs finally calm down, I raise my eyes back to the man's face only to find his smile gone, replaced by the empty dullness of death.

In panic, I scour my memory for what the man had said. Something about a name... Doctor Ebonee? Jebon... Jebonee? Doctor Lebone? Try as I might, I can't recall the words more accurately or even the meaning behind them.

As I ponder, my right eye comes alive and allows me another window into my surroundings. Now with a bit more confidence in my ability to handle all that I'm sensing, I turn my attention to what I see with the eye. The world it shows seems oddly more tinted in green with something obscuring most of its vision. I bring my arm to the obstruction and try to move it aside. It is a flat, uneven piece of something that feels soft on the other side and hard on the other. Moving it sends a sudden jolt of pain through the side of my face. I am abruptly gripped by panic, and I lose control of my thoughts for an instant.

I force my eyes closed, or try to. The treacherous right eye, which earlier refused to see anything, now refuses to go dark. Substituting the eyelid with my hand, I focus on my breathing instead. The stream of words from earlier, still visible in the darkness, has come to a halt. I feel like it is waiting for something, an input, almost demanding it from me. I make a quick scan through all the text... Wait a second. How could I read this? I can't read!

The words become a jumble of indecipherable symbols for a few moments before I am able to read them again... Well, that was odd... I clearly can read... So, why did I suddenly believe I couldn't? I push the pointless mystery aside for another, less distressing, time and concentrate on the massive list of phrases floating in my vision.

Somehow, I am able to quickly remove most of the incomprehensible babble from the litany of text. Some phrases refuse to obey my instinctive commands, however.

System scan results:

Fatal memory leak detected... (critical: reinitialize system before memory is full / current memory usage 12%)

Spinal storage of cerebrum vitae low...

Component: Spinal purifier unattached or missing... (critical: only use purified vitae)

Component: Face panel fastened incorrectly...

Component: Left arm unattached or missing...

Component: Left leg unattached or missing...

Component: Right leg support cage unattached or missing... (muscle strength limited until attached)

Most of those flash in bright red, apparently trying to remind me I should take them seriously.

The remaining list concludes after a few more lines:

Organic scan results:

Bleeding near organic brain... (attempting purge through tear duct)

Unbalanced hormone levels... (expect rapid mood swings)

Nutrient levels below advised limits...

Is this all meant to make sense to me somehow? I blink in confusion—well, at least I blink in my mind as my left eye is closed and the right one doesn't seem to abide by my will. The bleeding near my brain would explain the headache at least. What comes to the rest of the ominous threats seemingly looming over me, in my still disoriented state, I am only able to attest to feeling ill and weaker than I believe I should.

I take a deep breath. I am alone with, what I feel is, very limited time. I have to save myself if I intend to survive. It terrifies me to acknowledge this to myself. I push back the rising panic once more, clench my jaw, and resolve not to succumb to the disorientation caused by my senses.

I cautiously open my left eye and take my hand off the right one. The flat object is still obscuring my vision somewhat, but I don't want to continue fiddling with it before I know why moving it hurts me.

I turn myself on my back with some small effort and start examining my own body.

The right arm I've been using to crawl on the floor seems outwards, almost like a normal human arm with several sturdy metal supports from wrist to shoulder, fastened tightly around the arm with both metal and leather straps. What truly differentiates the arm from normal is two large openings in the flesh of the wrist and the elbow fold. Small brass gears and tendons move inside the openings, matching the movements I make with my arm... I am struck by an unexpected feeling of revulsion as the tendons move in and out of the flesh on the edges of the holes. However, the feeling is quickly pushed aside by curiosity towards the intricate workings of the little mechanisms.

Why did I feel revulsion towards my own body? I question myself before forcing this new pointless thought aside and continue my inspection.

I follow the tendons as they move under the skin of my hand. Now that I concentrate on it, I can indeed feel the slight pull of the metal wires inside the flesh of my fingers as I open and close my fist. I turn my hand around and look more closely at the fingers, trying to figure out how much underneath is metal and how much is flesh. After staring for a while, I manage to only discern that, other than the small brass claws embedded into the fingertips, the hand as a whole looks very much like a normal human hand... Hmm... How do I know what normal is? It must be because the dead man's hands are like this, just larger... and he is or... was a human, right?
